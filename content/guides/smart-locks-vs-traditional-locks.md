"Should I add smart locks to my range?" is one of the most common questions hardware wholesalers ask — and the honest answer is: yes, but not instead of traditional locks. The two categories serve different buyers, different price points and different failure modes. This guide compares them across the dimensions that matter for your buying decision.

## The Market in One Paragraph

Traditional mechanical locks (mortise, cylindrical knob and lever locks, padlocks, cam locks) remain the volume backbone of the category: universal demand, stable pricing, no installation complexity. Smart locks are the growth and margin story — electronic deadbolts, fingerprint padlocks and cabinet locks driven by Airbnb hosts, rental management and office upgrades — but they demand more from sellers: support, batteries, app ecosystems and warranty handling.

## Head-to-Head Comparison

### Unit cost and margin

| | Traditional | Smart |
|---|---|---|
| Typical wholesale (entry) | $1–5 | $12–35 |
| Typical retail markup | 1.5–2.5× | 2.5–4× |
| Price pressure | High (commodity) | Moderate (still differentiating) |

Smart locks cost more to stock but the absolute profit per unit is usually higher — one sold smart lock can equal the profit of ten padlocks.

### Failure rates and returns

- **Mechanical locks fail slowly and visibly**: keys get harder to turn, latches misalign. Warranty claims are rare and usually mechanical adjustments.
- **Smart locks fail electronically**: dead batteries (the #1 support call), app pairing issues, fingerprint reader wear, motor failures. Expect 2–5% warranty/return rates and budget customer-service time accordingly.

**Wholesale tip:** Insist on battery-low alarms and mechanical key backup (or emergency external power via USB/9V) in any smart lock you stock — it eliminates most support tickets.

### Installation and target buyers

- Traditional locks: universal — every door, every locksmith, every DIY customer. Mortise cylinders and euro cylinders are among the fastest-moving SKUs in the entire hardware category.
- Smart locks: require a standard bore-hole deadbolt prep in most cases, some comfort with setup apps, and Wi-Fi/BLE at the door. Best buyers: rental hosts, small offices, gyms/lockers, higher-end residential.

## Which Smart Lock Features Actually Sell

If you do stock smart locks, feature priorities observed across markets:

1. **Fingerprint (biometric) unlocking** — the killer feature; instant, no app needed, no code to remember
2. **Mechanical key backup** — trust factor for first-time buyers
3. **Mobile app + temporary codes** — essential for the Airbnb/rental segment
4. **Battery life 6–12 months** with low-battery warning
5. **Weather resistance (IP54+)** for exterior doors

Features that add cost but rarely drive purchase: cameras in locks, voice-assistant integration (nice for marketing, weak in practice), remote auto-unlock geofencing.

## Categories Worth Stocking Together

Smart and traditional lines actually cross-sell:

- **Smart padlocks + traditional padlocks** — same shelf, buyer chooses by use case (fingerprint padlocks are booming for gyms, schools, toolboxes)
- **Smart deadbolts + heavy-duty traditional deadbolts** — retrofits and new builds
- **Cabinet/cam locks** — traditional cam locks still dominate furniture and mailboxes; electronic cabinet locks are an emerging office/healthcare niche
- **Door hardware spares** — handles, hinges and cylinders ride along with every lock sale

## Regional Observations for Wholesale Buyers

- **North America:** deadbolt form factor (single-cylinder standard), Grade 2/3 expectations, growing smart adoption in single-family rentals
- **Europe:** euro-profile cylinders dominate; GDPR questions around app-connected devices occasionally come up — choose locks with local BLE-only modes
- **Southeast Asia / Middle East / Africa:** value mechanical locks and fingerprint padlocks; avoid Wi-Fi-dependent models where connectivity is unreliable
- **Latin America:** strong mortise lock demand; security perception favors visible heft

## A Balanced Starter Assortment

For a mixed hardware business testing the smart category:

1. Fingerprint padlock, IP54, USB-C charging — low entry price, high wow factor
2. One smart deadbolt with fingerprint + key backup + app codes
3. Established mechanical lines: mortise locks, euro/knob locksets, laminated padlocks, cam locks
4. Cylinders and hardware spares to capture replacement demand

This mix lets you sell the smart story without betting the catalog on electronics.
