Fasteners look like the simplest product in a hardware catalog — until you try to order them. Metric or SAE? Grade 8.8 or 12.9? Zinc plated or hot-dip galvanized? The wrong choice leaves you with stock that nobody buys, while the right assortment turns fasteners into a steady, high-repeat business: customers who buy bolts come back for nuts, washers, anchors, and everything else.

This guide covers the essentials every fastener buyer should know.

## Metric vs. SAE (Imperial)

Two measurement systems, two markets:

- **Metric (M6, M8, M10, M12...)** — used almost everywhere except the United States. Bolt diameter is the "M" number in millimeters; length is measured in millimeters from under the head.
- **SAE / Imperial (1/4", 5/16", 3/8"...)** — standard in the US market, and common in older machinery worldwide.

**Wholesale tip:** If your market is anywhere outside North America, lead with metric. Popular retail ranges are M5–M12 in lengths from 16 mm to 120 mm. For the US market, 1/4"–1/2" is the fast-moving range.

## Understanding Strength Grades

Bolt grade markings tell you tensile strength — and price:

**Metric bolts (property class):**

| Class | Marking | Typical use | Relative cost |
|-------|---------|-------------|---------------|
| 4.8 | | Light-duty, furniture, general | Low |
| 8.8 | 8.8 | Structural, automotive, machinery | Mid |
| 10.9 | 10.9 | High-stress, engine, suspension | Higher |
| 12.9 | 12.9 | Professional/industrial fastening | Highest |

**SAE bolts (grade lines on the head):** Grade 2 (no marks) = general purpose; Grade 5 (3 marks) = automotive/machinery; Grade 8 (6 marks) = high strength.

**Rule of thumb for assortment buying:** 8.8 is the sweet spot for most markets — strong enough for serious work, affordable enough for retail pricing. Nuts should match or exceed the bolt class.

## Washers: The Small Part That Matters

- **Flat washers** — spread the load and protect the surface. The default in any assortment.
- **Fender washers** — extra-wide bearing surface for soft materials (plastic, wood, thin sheet).
- **Spring lock washers (split)** — resist loosening from vibration.
- **Tooth lock washers** — stronger vibration resistance; used in electrical and automotive assemblies.
- **Nylon insert lock nuts** — the most reliable anti-loosening option; pairs with any bolt in the assortment.

## Surface Finishes and Corrosion Protection

Finish affects both appearance and how the fastener survives your customer's climate:

- **Zinc plated (blue-white or yellow)** — the default export finish; good indoor and light outdoor protection at minimal cost.
- **Hot-dip galvanized (HDG)** — thick coating for outdoor, coastal and marine use. Essential for construction-market assortments.
- **Black oxide** — decorative, mild protection; popular for furniture and interior hardware.
- **Dacromet / geometric coating** — salt-spray resistance of 500–1000+ hours; specify for automotive and harsh-environment buyers.

**Climate tip:** For humid coastal markets (Southeast Asia, island nations, coastal Latin America), upgrade zinc plating thickness or offer an HDG line — it is a genuine selling point against cheap competitors.

## Building an Assortment That Sells

The fastest-moving retail format is the **boxed or blister-packed assortment**: one SKU covering a range of sizes. Proven configurations:

1. **Hex bolt & nut kits** — M5/M6/M8 with matching nuts and washers, 100–300 pieces
2. **Washer sets** — flat + lock washers in a divided box
3. **Furniture bolt kits** — with barrel nuts and dowel screws for flat-pack furniture repair
4. **Anchor assortments** — expansion anchors with matching screws (always include the screw!)

## Quality Checks Before You Commit

- Thread check: a matching nut should run the full thread length smoothly by hand
- Head stamping: grade markings crisp and legible (fake grade marks are common in low-end supply)
- Coating uniformity: no bare spots on thread crests
- Sample salt-spray test if your market is coastal — ask the supplier for test reports
