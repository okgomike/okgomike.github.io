  Angular contact ball bearings feature raceways on both the inner and outer rings that are offset relative to each other in the axial direction, creating a contact angle between the ball and the raceway. This design enables the bearing to support combined radial and axial loads simultaneously, with the axial load capacity increasing as the contact angle increases. The LD-403 series is available with standard contact angles of 15 degrees (C type), 25 degrees (AC type), 30 degrees (B type), and 40 degrees (A type), allowing selection based on the specific ratio of radial to axial load in the application.

  The LD-403 series covers the 7000, 7200, 7300, and 7400 series, representing ultra-light, light, medium, and heavy-duty configurations. Bore diameters range from 10mm to 100mm to suit a wide variety of shaft sizes. These bearings are designed for high-speed operation with low friction and heat generation, making them ideal for applications such as machine tool spindles, pumps, compressors, and electric motors. The precision-ground raceways and balls ensure smooth, quiet operation with minimal vibration.

  For applications requiring even higher speeds or electrical insulation, ceramic hybrid versions with silicon nitride (Si3N4) balls are available. Ceramic balls are significantly lighter than steel balls, reducing centrifugal force and friction at high speeds, while also providing superior corrosion resistance and electrical insulation properties. Cage options include nylon (PA66) for quiet high-speed operation, machined brass for heavy-duty and high-temperature applications, and stamped steel for general-purpose use. The LD-403 series is available in ABEC 3 through ABEC 7 precision grades for applications ranging from industrial machinery to ultra-high-precision equipment.

  Angular contact ball bearings are frequently used in paired or matched set arrangements to handle axial loads in both directions and to provide increased rigidity. Common arrangements include back-to-back (DB) for maximum moment stiffness, face-to-face (DF) for easier installation and tolerance to misalignment, and tandem (DT) for increased axial load capacity in a single direction. Factory-matched sets are available with controlled preload or clearance specifications to ensure optimal performance without requiring on-site adjustment.

faqdata:
  - question: "How do I choose the right contact angle for my application?"
    answer: "Contact angle selection depends on the ratio of axial load to radial load. 15-degree contact angle (C type) is best for predominantly radial loads with light axial loads and offers the highest speed capability. 25-degree (AC type) is the most versatile general-purpose choice for moderate combined loads. 30-degree (B type) handles higher axial loads. 40-degree (A type) is for heavy axial loads and lower speeds. As a general rule, larger contact angles provide greater axial load capacity but reduced speed ratings."
  - question: "What are the different paired mounting arrangements and when should I use each?"
    answer: "There are three main paired arrangements: Back-to-back (DB) mounting has the wide ends of the outer rings facing each other, providing the greatest moment rigidity and resistance to misalignment, making it ideal for machine tool spindles. Face-to-face (DF) mounting has the narrow ends facing each other, which is more tolerant of misalignment and easier to install but provides lower moment stiffness. Tandem (DT) mounting has both bearings facing the same direction, doubling the axial load capacity in one direction and used when a single bearing cannot handle the axial load."
  - question: "What are the benefits of ceramic hybrid angular contact bearings?"
    answer: "Ceramic hybrid bearings use silicon nitride (Si3N4) balls instead of steel balls, offering several advantages: 40% lower weight reduces centrifugal force and skidding at high speeds, enabling 30-50% higher maximum speed. Ceramic balls have higher hardness and smoother surfaces for longer service life. They are also electrically insulating, preventing electrical discharge damage in motor applications. Additionally, ceramic balls have lower thermal expansion, providing more stable preload across temperature variations."
  - question: "What is the difference between preloaded and clearance angular contact bearings?"
    answer: "Preloaded bearings have a negative internal clearance, meaning the balls are under slight compression between the raceways even at rest. Preload increases bearing stiffness, reduces deflection under load, and improves rotational accuracy, making it essential for precision applications like machine tool spindles. Clearance (C1-C5) bearings have positive internal clearance and are used in applications where thermal expansion needs to be accommodated or where the load is primarily radial. Factory-matched sets are available with specific preload classes (light, medium, heavy) for consistent performance."
