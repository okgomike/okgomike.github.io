  - question: "How do I use an adjustable wrench correctly?"
    answer: "Place the wrench on the fastener with the fixed jaw on the side toward which you will pull. This puts the load on the stronger fixed jaw. Adjust the movable jaw until it fits snugly on the fastener. Always pull toward the fixed jaw side, not the adjustable jaw side, to prevent the jaw from loosening and slipping."
  - question: "What is the difference between chrome vanadium and carbon steel wrenches?"
    answer: "Chrome vanadium (Cr-V) steel has higher strength, better toughness, and superior wear resistance compared to carbon steel. Cr-V wrenches maintain their dimensional precision longer, resist jaw wear, and are less likely to break under load. The chrome plating on Cr-V steel also provides better corrosion resistance than the black oxide finish typical of carbon steel wrenches."
  - question: "Can I use an adjustable wrench for torque-critical applications?"
    answer: "Adjustable wrenches are not recommended for torque-critical applications where precise tightening force is required. The adjustable jaw can slip under high torque, potentially damaging the fastener. For torque-critical work, use a fixed-size wrench or socket with a torque wrench. Adjustable wrenches are best for general-purpose work where convenience is more important than precision."
