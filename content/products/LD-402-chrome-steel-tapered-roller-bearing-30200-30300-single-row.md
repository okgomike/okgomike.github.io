faqdata:
  - question: "What is the difference between the cone and cup in a tapered roller bearing?"
    answer: "A tapered roller bearing consists of two main separable components: the cone (inner ring assembly) and the cup (outer ring). The cone includes the inner ring, tapered rollers, and cage assembled together. The cup is the outer ring that the rollers roll against. This separable design allows each component to be installed independently, and the bearing clearance or preload is adjusted by controlling how far the cone is seated into the cup."
  - question: "How do I properly mount and preload a tapered roller bearing?"
    answer: "Tapered roller bearings are typically mounted in opposed pairs (face-to-face or back-to-back) to handle axial loads in both directions. Proper preload is achieved by tightening the mounting nut or adjusting shims until the desired preload is reached. Too little preload causes play and reduced stiffness, while excessive preload leads to overheating and premature failure. Always follow the manufacturer's recommended preload procedure and torque specifications for your specific application."
  - question: "What is the difference between 30200 and 30300 series tapered roller bearings?"
    answer: "The 30200 series is the standard medium-duty tapered roller bearing with a contact angle of approximately 12-15 degrees. The 30300 series is the heavy-duty version with wider rollers, a steeper contact angle (approximately 25-30 degrees), and significantly higher radial and axial load capacity. Both series have the same bore diameter for a given bearing number, but the 30300 series has a larger outer diameter and wider width. Choose the 30300 series for applications with heavier loads or higher shock conditions."
  - question: "Can tapered roller bearings handle thrust loads in both directions?"
    answer: "A single-row tapered roller bearing can only handle axial (thrust) load in one direction. For bidirectional axial loads, two tapered roller bearings must be mounted in opposition, either face-to-face (DF arrangement) or back-to-back (DB arrangement). Back-to-back mounting provides greater moment rigidity and is more common in wheel hub applications. Double-row tapered roller bearings (32200/32300 series) are designed to handle both radial and bidirectional axial loads in a single assembly."
