faqdata:
  - question: "What is the difference between 6-point and 12-point sockets?"
    answer: "6-point sockets provide maximum contact area with the fastener, gripping the flat sides of the hex head. This reduces the risk of rounding off fasteners, especially under high torque or on worn fasteners. 12-point sockets can engage at 30-degree intervals for better access in tight spaces but grip the corners of the fastener, increasing rounding risk. For automotive work, 6-point sockets are strongly recommended. The LD-266 set uses 6-point sockets for maximum durability."
  - question: "Which drive size do I use for lug nuts?"
    answer: "Most automotive lug nuts require a 1/2-inch drive socket in 17mm, 19mm, 21mm, or 22mm size depending on the vehicle. The 1/2-inch drive provides the strength needed for the high torque (80-140 ft-lbs) required for lug nuts. Never use a 3/8-inch drive for lug nuts as it may break under the torque. Always use a torque wrench for final tightening to ensure proper and even lug nut torque."
  - question: "What is the swing arc and why does it matter?"
    answer: "The swing arc is the minimum angle the ratchet must rotate to engage the next tooth and produce a turning action. A 72-tooth ratchet has a 5-degree swing arc, meaning you only need 5 degrees of rotation to turn the fastener. This is critical in tight spaces where you may only have a small range of motion. Lower-tooth ratchets (e.g., 36-tooth with 10-degree arc) require more swing room. The LD-266 set uses 72-tooth ratchets for maximum accessibility."

Periodically inspect sockets for cracks or deformation and replace damaged pieces immediately."
