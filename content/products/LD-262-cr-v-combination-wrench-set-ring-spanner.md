faqdata:
  - question: "What is the advantage of a 12-point box-end over a 6-point?"
    answer: "A 12-point box-end can engage the fastener at 30-degree intervals, allowing you to turn the fastener in tighter spaces where only a 30-degree swing is available. A 6-point box-end requires a 60-degree swing. However, 6-point box-ends provide better contact and are less likely to round off fasteners under high torque. For most automotive and mechanical applications, 12-point is preferred for its accessibility advantage."
  - question: "Why choose a combination wrench over separate open-end and box-end wrenches?"
    answer: "A combination wrench gives you both fastening options in a single tool. Use the open-end for quick engagement and removal of loose fasteners, then switch to the box-end for final tightening or breaking loose stubborn fasteners. This saves time and reduces the number of tools needed. The combination design is the most popular wrench style for professional mechanics."
  - question: "Can I use these wrenches with an extension or cheater bar?"
    answer: "Using a cheater bar (pipe extension) on the wrench handle to increase leverage is not recommended. The wrench is designed for hand-applied torque only. Excessive leverage can exceed the wrench's yield strength, causing permanent bending or breakage, and can also damage the fastener. For stubborn fasteners, use a longer wrench or a breaker bar with a socket. Using penetrating oil and heat is safer than excessive leverage."
  - question: "What is the difference between Cr-V and S2 steel for wrenches?"
    answer: "Chrome vanadium (Cr-V) steel is the standard material for wrenches, offering excellent strength, toughness, and wear resistance at a reasonable cost. S2 steel is harder and is typically used for screwdriver bits and hex keys where maximum hardness is needed. For wrenches, Cr-V is preferred because it offers better toughness (resistance to breaking) while still providing adequate hardness for fastening applications."
