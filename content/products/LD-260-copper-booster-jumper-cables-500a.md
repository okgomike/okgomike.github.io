  - question: "How do I safely jump-start a vehicle with these cables?"
    answer: "Connect the RED clamp to the positive (+) terminal of the dead battery, then the other RED clamp to the positive (+) terminal of the good battery. Connect the BLACK clamp to the negative (-) terminal of the good battery, then the other BLACK clamp to an unpainted metal surface on the engine block or chassis of the dead vehicle (ground connection). Start the donor vehicle, wait 2-3 minutes, then start the dead vehicle. Remove cables in reverse order. Never connect both black clamps directly to battery negative terminals to avoid explosion risk from hydrogen gas."
  - question: "What is the difference between 500A and 1000A booster cables?"
    answer: "The amperage rating indicates the maximum current the cables can safely carry. 500A cables are suitable for most passenger cars and SUVs with 12V batteries up to about 70Ah. 1000A cables are needed for larger vehicles like trucks, SUVs with large batteries, or diesel engines that require more starting current. 2000A cables are for heavy-duty commercial trucks and equipment. Using cables with insufficient amperage rating for the vehicle may result in slow charging or cable overheating."
  - question: "What cable gauge (AWG) should I choose?"
    answer: "Lower AWG numbers indicate thicker cables that can carry more current. For passenger cars, 8-10 AWG is sufficient for occasional use. For SUVs and light trucks, 6-8 AWG is recommended. For trucks and commercial vehicles, use 4-6 AWG or thicker. Thicker cables (lower AWG) provide faster charging and less voltage drop, resulting in quicker and more reliable jump starts. Always choose cables rated for at least the starting current of your largest vehicle."
  - question: "Pure copper or copper-clad aluminum (CCA) - which should I choose?"
    answer: "Pure copper cables have lower electrical resistance, better conductivity, and superior flexibility compared to CCA cables. This means pure copper cables charge faster, stay cooler, and are easier to handle in cold weather. CCA cables are less expensive but have higher resistance, leading to slower charging and more heating. For regular or professional use, pure copper is strongly recommended. For occasional emergency use, CCA may be acceptable for passenger vehicles."
