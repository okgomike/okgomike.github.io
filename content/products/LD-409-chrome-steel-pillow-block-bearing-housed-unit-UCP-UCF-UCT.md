faqdata:
  - question: "How do I choose between UCP, UCF, and UCT housing types?"
    answer: "Choose UCP (pillow block) for general purpose shaft support where the bearing mounts on a flat horizontal surface and provides the most stable base. Choose UCF (4-bolt flange) when mounting to a vertical surface or where space is limited and a compact footprint is desired. Choose UCT (tapped base) when you need adjustable shaft positioning or when mounting to surfaces where through-bolts are impractical. UCFL (2-bolt flange) is used for lighter loads in tight spaces, and UCPA offers adjustable pillow block positioning."
  - question: "What is the difference between set screw locking and eccentric locking collar?"
    answer: "Set screw locking uses two hardened set screws that bite into the shaft to secure the bearing inner ring. It is simple, cost-effective, and suitable for moderate loads and speeds. Eccentric locking collar uses an eccentric collar that rotates to wedge the bearing onto the shaft, providing a more secure grip with better concentricity. Eccentric locking is preferred for higher speeds, heavier loads, vibration-prone applications, and where frequent shaft removal and reinstallation is needed, as it causes less shaft damage than set screws."
  - question: "Can housed bearing units accommodate shaft misalignment?"
    answer: "Yes, housed bearing units with spherical outer ring insert bearings (like the UC series inserts in LD-409) are designed to accommodate shaft misalignment. The spherical outer diameter of the insert bearing pivots within the spherical bore of the housing, allowing typically 2 to 3 degrees of angular misalignment. This compensates for shaft deflection, mounting inaccuracies, and housing misalignment. However, excessive misalignment beyond rated limits can cause accelerated seal wear and reduced bearing life."
  - question: "How often should pillow block bearings be lubricated?"
    answer: "Lubrication intervals depend on operating conditions including speed, load, temperature, and environment. Under normal conditions (moderate speed, normal temperature, clean environment), re-greasing every 6 to 12 months or 2000-4000 operating hours is typical. For harsh environments with dust, moisture, or high temperatures, more frequent lubrication (every 1 to 3 months) may be needed. Most pillow block housings have grease fittings for convenient re-lubrication without disassembly. Always use the recommended grease type and quantity."
