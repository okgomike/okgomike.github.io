  - question: "Why use a wooden mallet instead of a metal hammer for chisels?"
    answer: "Wooden mallets are preferred for chisel work because they deliver a cushioned blow that is less likely to damage wooden chisel handles. The larger striking surface of the cylindrical head allows you to focus on the chisel without watching the mallet. Wooden mallets also provide better feel and control for carving work. Metal hammers can split, mushroom, or damage chisel handles and provide too much shock for delicate work."
  - question: "What weight mallet do I need for carving?"
    answer: "For delicate carving and detail work, an 8-12oz (225-340g) mallet is ideal. For general woodworking and furniture assembly, a 16oz (450g) mallet is the most versatile choice. For heavy mortise chisel work and large joint assembly, a 20-24oz (570-680g) mallet provides the necessary force. Carvers often use multiple mallet weights for different stages of work."
  - question: "How do I maintain a wooden mallet?"
    answer: "Keep the mallet clean and dry. Periodically apply a light coat of linseed oil or beeswax to protect the wood and prevent drying and cracking. If the striking surface becomes dented or compressed, it can be sanded smooth with fine sandpaper. If the handle becomes loose, tap it tighter or add a wedge. Store in a dry environment away from direct heat sources that can cause the wood to dry and crack. Avoid using the mallet on metal surfaces."
  - question: "Will the wooden mallet damage my chisel handles?"
    answer: "No, the wooden mallet is specifically designed to be used with chisels. The wood-on-wood contact is gentler than metal-on-wood and will not split or mushroom the chisel handle. However, if the chisel handle is already damaged or split, the mallet may accelerate the damage. Always inspect chisel handles before use and replace damaged handles. The mallet is designed for use with wooden-handled chisels; for plastic-handled chisels, a rubber mallet may be more appropriate."
